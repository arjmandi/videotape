# Player

TapePlayer is the macOS app a person uses to click through a rendered tape as if it were the app. From the repo root, `open clients/macos/build/TapePlayer.app --args examples/contrail/tapes/terminal` opens one window in the tape's shape, titled with the tape's label from `tape.json`'s `made` block, such as `contrail 0.1.0 + terminal 1.0.0`. It plays the opening and rests on the home key. While a state rests, each hotspot is an invisible area that shows a pointing hand and that accessibility sees as a button named by its label. After the buttons, accessibility sees one static text per entry of the state's `words`, in reading order, so a screen reader reads every word on screen. Clicking a hotspot plays its move into the next state. On a state with a search bar that has options, a typed printable key or a click on the bar opens the platform's text sheet: the bar's placeholder over one text field, with the typed key already in it. Return picks the one option whose label the text names, ignoring case, as the whole label or as the start of a word in it, and plays its move. No match, or more than one, plays the bar's no-match move. Escape closes the sheet and stays. Back (⌘[ or Playback > Back) plays the move that arrived backward and rests on the state it left. Repeated Back walks further back. Back at the entry, or while a clip plays, does nothing, and the menu item is disabled then. Start Over (⌘R) plays from the beginning and forgets the history, and File > Open picks another tape. About TapePlayer shows the tape's label and id. The app draws nothing of its own over the tape but the sheet, the menus, the pointer and accessibility.

The UI tests, `player-cuts` and the journeys a person drives have not been driven on a tape of the new shape yet, because each takes over the screen and needs the user's yes, and because the engine writes that shape. `player-build` and `player-press` have been driven on TapeKit's `search` fixture. The `reads [...]` lists below come from Contrail's screenplay, not from a rendered tape. Correct any expected line below that the first drive proves wrong.

## Sub-features

- `player-build` builds `clients/macos/build/TapePlayer.app`.
- `player-press` drives a tape through TapeKit's loader and player without a window: presses hotspots by label, types into the search bar, goes back, and names what the resting state offers and reads.
- `player-walk` is a UI test that plans a walk from the tape's `tape.json` and drives it: every hotspot of every state at least once, `sea`, `honolulu` and `san` typed into the search bar, Escape on the sheet, Back at the entry and from at least five states, with the state's name and words checked at every landing.
- `player-booking` goes home, `Where to?`, search, `Seattle (SEA)`, the dates, `Saturday, December 12, $89`, that day's flights, the 8:15 AM flight, `Book for $89`, the booking, `Done`, and home again, and the window then shows the home key.
- `player-typing` opens the text sheet from a typed key or a click on the bar, and Return plays the option the text names or the no-match move.
- `player-back` rewinds the move that arrived and rests where it left, further back on each press, and does nothing at the entry.
- `player-words` reads the state's words to the screen reader after its buttons.
- `player-title` titles the window with the tape's label, and About shows the label and id.
- `player-hotspots` shows a pointing hand and accessibility buttons over hotspots only while a state rests, and ignores clicks while a clip plays.
- `player-start-over` plays the opening again on ⌘R, mid-clip or at rest, and forgets the history.
- `player-open` shows the Open panel when launched without a tape, and File > Open (⌘O) switches to a tape folder or its `tape.json`.
- `player-bad-tape` shows an alert headed `This tape cannot play.` with the reason, and File > Open still works after it.
- `player-window` keeps the tape's shape through resizing and zoom with the video reaching every edge.
- `player-cuts` cuts from clip to clip with no blank, black or half-faded frame between them.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Render the tape with `uv run videotape render --story terminal` in `examples/contrail`, build with `clients/macos/build.sh`, then run `open clients/macos/build/TapePlayer.app --args examples/contrail/tapes/terminal` from the repo root.
- Open `clients/macos/build/TapePlayer.app` with no arguments and pick a tape in the Open panel.
- In a running TapePlayer, click a hotspot, type into a search bar, or choose Playback > Back (⌘[), Playback > Start Over (⌘R), File > Open… (⌘O) or TapePlayer > About TapePlayer.
- With VoiceOver on (⌘F5), move through the window with VO-Right Arrow.

## Gotchas

- The UI tests and every journey a person drives take over the real mouse and screen. Ask the user before running them. Nobody should touch the mouse or keyboard while `player-test` runs, since the walk types into the sheet.
- The walk's `reads` checks compare the window's static texts with `words` in `tape.json`, so they prove the player against the tape, not the tape against the screenplay. Whether `words` is right is the render's job.
- The `reads [...]` lists in this file were derived from the screenplay before any tape of the new shape was rendered. The engine's `tape.json` is the source. On the first drive, copy the lists from `$RUN/player-press.stdout` into this file where they differ, and say so in the report.
- `player-press` proves what the tape offers, where each press, typed text and Back lands, and what the state reads, through the code TapePlayer loads and plays with. It does not prove the window, the clips on screen, the sheet, the pointer or accessibility. Those need `player-test` and a person.
- `player-press` builds a small Swift package in `.claude/skills/verify/scripts/player-press/` against `clients/macos/TapeKit`, into `clients/macos/build/player-press`. A change to TapeKit's `Tape` or `Player` can break that build. Then its exit is `2`.
- A label argument to `player-press` that starts with `--` is read as `--type` or `--back`. No Contrail label does.
- To compile the UI tests without running them, run `xcodebuild build-for-testing -quiet -project TapePlayer.xcodeproj -scheme TapePlayer -destination "platform=macOS,arch=$(uname -m)" -derivedDataPath build/DerivedData` in `clients/macos`. It exits 0 and opens nothing.
- Arguments after `player-test "$RUN"` go to `xcodebuild`, so `-only-testing:TapePlayerUITests/TapePlayerUITests/<test>` runs one UI test. The unit tests run every time.
- The walk takes 154 clips of about a second each plus the test's own waits, so expect several minutes. It stops at the first failed step and names the step, such as `step 12 of 156, press "Chicago (ORD)" from search(): rests on no_flights(ORD)`, so read the first failure, not the last.
- The walk plans from `tape.json` as rendered. A hotspot the checks did not find is not in `tape.json` and is not walked, and an option without a found box is typed, not clicked. On a Wan tape, read the render's `fail:` lines first.
- Both `Where to?` labels look alike. Home's search bar has no options, so its hotspot plays into search() like any button. The bar on search() has options, so its hotspot opens the sheet, and `player-press` reports `FAIL search() asks for text at "Where to?"` when told to press it.
- Typing matches the start of a word, so `bos)` picks `Boston (BOS)` through the `(` boundary, and `new` picks `New York (JFK)` on the Contrail tape because it is the only option that starts with `New`. An empty field plays the no-match move.
- Back plays the clip backward by stepping a frame per tick at the tape's fps, not by AVPlayer's reverse rate, which dropped a quarter of the stand-in's H.264 frames. A rewind takes the clip's length plus one tick, and `player-cuts` never goes back, so its cuts are all forward.
- A key with ⌘ or ⌃ is a shortcut, not text, and a function or arrow key is not text. A key typed while a clip plays, or on a state without a search bar, goes nowhere.
- `open` launches TapePlayer with `/` as its working directory. TapePlayer resolves a relative tape path against `/` first, then against each folder above the app bundle. So `examples/contrail/tapes/terminal` works only for a copy of the app inside this checkout. Pass an absolute path to any other copy.
- The Open panel hides folders whose names start with a dot, so a checkout under one, such as `~/.t3/projects/`, cannot be browsed to. Press ⌘⇧G in the panel and paste the absolute path of the tape folder or its `tape.json`.
- `open ... --args` is ignored when TapePlayer is already running. `open` only brings the running window forward. Quit TapePlayer (⌘Q) first.
- The UI tests play `examples/contrail/tapes/terminal` as it is on disk. No script renders it. If it is missing, the tests fail and name the render command, `uv run videotape render --story terminal` in `examples/contrail`. After a screenplay, data or story edit, render again before testing. A tape of the old shape, without `words`, `search` or `made.label`, does not load: TapePlayer shows the bad-tape alert, and the walk fails to decode `tape.json`.
- The stand-in's two tapes look alike on screen, since the stand-in draws only the words, which a story does not change. They differ in the faint row of cells along the bottom edge of each key, and in the window's title.
- A UI test run needs macOS Automation Mode. On a Mac where `automationmodetool` prints `This device requires user authentication to enable Automation Mode.`, each run asks the user for their password. Unanswered, it fails after a minute with `Timed out while enabling automation mode.` and `Executed 0 tests`. The user can answer the prompt, or once run `automationmodetool enable-automationmode-without-authentication` with an admin password. Never run it yourself.
- On the prototype 1 tape, the screenshot's colours sat off the keys by 7.78 on that scale on the Dell P3223QE where it was measured. macOS decodes the untagged clips with the BT.709 matrix they were not encoded with, and the display's profile shifts them again. The booking test removes each image's mean colour, so it compares which key shows, not its exact colours.
- Window tiling (dragging to a screen edge, or Window > Move & Resize) ignores the tape's shape. The video then crops at two edges. That is expected and is not a `player-window` failure.
- The pointer turns into a hand as soon as a state comes to rest under it, and back into an arrow when a move starts, without the pointer moving. The UI tests can't read the cursor, so a person checks both cases by hand in the Hotspots journey.
- `player-cuts` builds its harness from TapePlayer's and TapeKit's Swift sources with `swiftc`, outside Xcode. A change to the player's view or playback code can break that build even when `player-build` passes. Then `player-cuts.exit` is `2`, and the harness in `.claude/skills/verify/scripts/player-cuts/` needs the same change. Its control projector carries TapePlayer's projector API, `rewind` included, for the same reason.
- `player-cuts` needs no Screen Recording permission, because a process may capture its own windows. It does not move the mouse, but its window plays on screen for about a minute on a stand-in tape, and proportionally longer for longer clips.
- The control projector proves the captures can see a flash. A flash is judged before capture coverage, so a capture stall during the control run no longer fails the pass. Only TapePlayer's clean verdict needs every cut densely sampled.
- An action the checks did not find on its key has no hotspot in `tape.json`, so TapePlayer shows no hand and no accessibility button for it, and the journey through it cannot be clicked. The render prints a `fail:` line for it and the Keys section of `dailies.html` lists it as `not found`. On a stand-in tape every action is found. On a Wan tape, read the `fail:` lines first and report such a journey as blocked by the model. A search option is still typed whether or not its box was found.

# Moves test

`uv run scripts/moves-test examples/contrail --stories terminal,posters --cap-usd 50`, run from the repo root, measures which of two ways of drawing an app's keys makes a move travel from one screen to the next as a camera would, with no cut and no dissolve. It takes twelve moves of Contrail, `MOVES` in the script, from `home()` to `search()` down to `flight(CT 300, 2026-12-12)` to `booked(CT 300, 2026-12-12)` and `my_trips()` to `trip(CT 100, 2026-12-18)`, and in each story draws them two ways. `pinned` is how a render draws today: each base key on its own, text to video, each variant from its base's picture, and each move first-and-last-frame between its two keys. `rehearse` draws the entry's key as today and every other scene's base key as the move that first reaches it in the walk: image to video from the key of the state it is reached from, with a prompt composed of the move's clauses, `It ends held still on the next screen.`, and the state's own clauses without `Static shot.`, so the clip is the move and its last frame is the key. A later move into a state is pinned, and a variant keeps its method and follows its base. Under each method it draws only the states the twelve moves need and what they depend on, the bases and, under rehearse, the chain of states each base is first reached through: 17 keys and 12 moves under pinned, 17 keys of which 9 rehearsed and 8 pinned moves under rehearse, per story. Each move's kept take gets the cut check, `VT506`, ffmpeg's scene score on every frame, failing over 0.3; the dissolve check, `VT507`, the model's judge shown eight frames spread evenly across the take in one request, saying of each whether it shows two pictures overlaid, failing when any does; and the seams, `VT508`. A rehearsed clip's key is its own last frame, so its end seam is 0 by construction, and the report says so rather than hiding it. Every key gets the readers' checks as a render makes them, and each variant the layout check `VT505`; a rehearsed key is drawn from another scene's picture, so no base layout holds it. Claude judges every model's moves but the stand-in's, whose own judge calls a frame overlaid when it is a blend of the clip's first and last frames, which the stand-in's crossfade makes its middle frames.

The ten labelled moves of 2026-11-10 calibrate both checks. They live outside the repo, at `examples/surf_shop/` in the user's main checkout, which git ignores, or wherever `--clips` says; `LABELS` in the script names each by its path under that folder and the check a person says it fails. Wan 2.2 5B's five under `tape/moves/` all dissolve. Of Wan 3.0's under `tape-wan3/moves/`, `welcome--enter-shop` walks to the door and then dissolves, `entrance--i-have-an-account` pushes in and then cuts, `entrance--create-an-account` dissolves, and `entrance--browse-as-guest` and `signup--sign-up` whip past a surfboard, which counts as a cut. Their scene scores cost nothing: the seven dissolves score 0.086 to 0.187 and the three cuts 0.319 to 0.381, so 0.3 agrees with all ten. Their judgments are paid. The report shows each clip's eight frames, the judge's answer on each, and where each check agrees with the labels. `--fixture <file>` writes the judge's recorded answers to them, text only, and `tests/test_moves_test.py` replays them against frames sampled live; the answers hold only for the question they answered, so a changed prompt or rule needs a new paid calibration.

Before any call it prints the estimate: a line per method and story with its shots, keys, rehearsals, pinned moves, calls and judgments, the calibration, and the model's calls at its price with the reads by Claude of each key's take and the judgments of each move and each labelled clip, at the most each costs, then the least and the most against the cap. It stops before any call when the least passes the cap, when no cap is set, or when an API key is missing. The tapes go into `tapes/moves-test/<method>/<story>/` in the project and the calibration into `tapes/moves-test/calibration/`, one cap holds for them all, and every call, read and judgment goes into the project's `ledger.jsonl`, a judgment as a line of kind `judgment` naming its shot and seed. Run again, it films no take on disk, reads no picture and judges no take whose answer is cached, so the report is rebuilt for free. It writes `tapes/moves-test/report.html`: the recommendation for each story, the calibration, the methods side by side, what one direction change would draw again under each, and each move under each method with the frames across its take, the judge's answer under each frame, its checks, the key it lands on and whether that key passes, its prompt and its cost, then the five chain clips under rehearse, and the sources. The recommendation for a story is the method with the most of the twelve moves that neither cut nor dissolve, then the most of their keys passing every check, then the lower cost, then the fewer keys and moves the direction change draws again. The bar is no cut and no dissolve on all twelve, and a method short of it is reported with its frames. `--model stand-in` runs the whole thing for free.

The estimate of 2026-11-18 for gemini-omni-flash-1.2 is 108 to 216 calls, $47.47 to $94.93, with 68 to 136 reads at most $11.74 to $23.47 and 68 judgments at most $8.31, $67.51 to $126.72 in all, past a cap of $50 at the least. The paid run of 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-19, under a cap the user raised to $70 and then $76, spent $74.66, with one fal HTTP 500 retaken. The calibration agreed 10 of 10 on both checks. Terminal: rehearse 7 of 12 moves travel, 6 of 12 keys pass, $16.99; pinned 4 of 12, with 3 cuts and whole-picture dissolves, 4 keys, $19.30. Posters: pinned 11 of 12, 4 keys, $19.51; rehearse 11 of 12, 3 keys, $18.38. One direction change draws 53 keys and 93 moves again under rehearse, 1 key and 12 moves under pinned. The user chose rehearse for render.

## Sub-features

- `moves-estimate` prints the selection, a line per method and story, the calibration line, the model's calls at its price with the reads and the judgments, and the least and the most against the cap. `--estimate` stops there with exit 0 and needs no API key.
- `moves-over-cap` stops with `VT402` before the first call when the least passes `--cap-usd`, and names the cap the run needs.
- `moves-no-cap` stops with `VT402` before the first call when no `--cap-usd` is given.
- `moves-no-key` stops with `VT104` before any call when `FAL_KEY` is missing, and when `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` is missing, which the readers and the judge need. Without the qwen extra it stops with `VT105` first.
- `moves-tests` is `uv run pytest tests/test_moves_test.py tests/test_checks.py tests/test_claude.py tests/test_standin.py tests/test_frames.py`, which runs the whole moves test on a stand-in with gemini-omni-flash-1.2's name and price, with Claude's reads and judgments through a fake network: both methods, the rehearsals and their composed prompts, the ledger, the rerun, one cap across the tapes, the report, the re-render counts and the recommendation, the fixture flag, and the two checks, the judge and the stand-in's judge on their own.
- `moves-calibration` is the two tests that hold the checks to the labels: `test_the_cut_check_agrees_with_the_labels_on_the_ten_clips` runs ffmpeg live on the clips, and `test_the_dissolve_check_agrees_with_the_labels_on_claudes_recorded_answers` replays `tests/fixtures/recorded/moves/calibration.json`. Both are skipped without the clips, and the second without the fixture.
- `moves-standin` is the free run with `--model stand-in` on a copy of examples/contrail, which writes a report with every move dissolving, since the stand-in crossfades every move.
- `moves-rerun` runs the same command again after a run, and films, reads and judges nothing.
- `moves-report` is what `report.html` must show.
- `moves-fixture` is `--fixture <file>` after the paid run, which writes Claude's recorded answers for the calibration test.
- `moves-paid` is the paid run on gemini-omni-flash-1.2. Costs money, and waits for the user's cap.
- `moves-explain` is `videotape explain VT506` and `videotape explain VT507`.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- From the repo root, read the estimate first: `uv run scripts/moves-test examples/contrail --stories terminal,posters --estimate`.
- Approve a cap at least the estimate's least, then run the same command with `--cap-usd <cap>` in place of `--estimate`. The cap stops a run before the call that would pass it. The run still writes the report from what was drawn and judged before it, shows a dissolve check the cap stopped as not judged, counts a move not drawn or judged as not travelling, and exits 1 with `VT402`. A run again with a higher cap resumes.
- Open `examples/contrail/tapes/moves-test/report.html` from disk. It needs no server.
- After the paid run, run the same command again with `--fixture tests/fixtures/recorded/moves/calibration.json`, which films, reads and judges nothing, writes the fixture, and lets `tests/test_moves_test.py` hold the dissolve check to the labels. Commit the fixture.
- Try it for free on a copy with `--model stand-in`, which needs no key and no cap, and `--clips <folder>` when the labelled clips are elsewhere.

## Gotchas

- The honest count is 108 calls at one take each, not the 75 the plan guessed: each story draws 17 keys and 12 moves under pinned and 25 shots under rehearse, and the bases the moves depend on, dates(HNL), results(HNL, 2026-12-11), flight(CT 500, 2026-12-11) and booked(CT 500, 2026-12-11), count under both. The least, $67.51, passes a $50 cap, so a paid run needs a higher cap or fewer stories.
- A rehearsal's prompt quotes the pressed words, as every move's does, and the stand-in letters every line a prompt quotes, so under the stand-in every rehearsed key but trip's shows the pressed words as an extra box and fails `VT503`, and the free run's recommendation leans on that artifact. A real model is hoped to read the move's words as direction; the paid run tells.
- Every stand-in move is a crossfade, so under the stand-in every move dissolves, no method meets the bar, and the recommendation falls through to the keys, the cost, and the re-renders. The stand-in's judge knows only its own crossfade: on the real labelled clips it sees no blend of the first and last frames and calls every frame one picture, so the free run's calibration shows `VT507 on 3 of 10`, which says nothing of Claude.
- ffmpeg's scene score measures how much the whole frame changes, so a cut between two screens that share a background and differ in a few lines scores low: a cut between two stand-in keys scores about 0.02. The threshold of 0.3 comes from the ten real clips, whose cuts change the whole picture.
- A judgment of eight 1280x720 frames is priced at the most it can cost, about $0.12, where the real spend sits nearer $0.05, like a read's.
- Under rehearse a base's clip is a key in the tape, `keys/<state>.png` and its take, and no `moves/<move>.mp4` is written for the move it rehearses; the report links the take. Its start seam is measured by the script against the key it leaves, and its end seam is 0 by construction.
- The direction change's counts under rehearse are large on purpose: a base's clip starts on its parent's key, so a change to the search scene's direction draws again search, every base first reached through it, their variants, and every move touching them, 53 keys and 93 moves of the terminal tape, against 1 key and 12 moves under pinned.
- The judgments are cached in each tape's `looks/` by the model, the prompt, the schema, the settings and the frames' hashes, beside the reads. A changed prompt misses the cache and pays again. The fixture carries `asked`, the hash of that question, and the calibration test refuses a fixture recorded for another.
- `--clips` keeps the path it is given; the default is `examples/surf_shop` under the repo root, which the user's main checkout has and a fresh worktree does not. Never copy the clips into the repo, and never commit `examples/surf_shop`.
- The tapes under `tapes/moves-test/` cost money after the paid run. Never delete them, their `takes/`, `looks/` or `frames/`, or their lines in `ledger.jsonl`, or a rerun pays again. A free run on a copy writes nothing into examples/contrail.

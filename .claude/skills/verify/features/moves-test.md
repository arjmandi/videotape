# Moves test

`uv run scripts/moves-test examples/contrail --cap-usd 5`, run from the repo root, judges the ten labelled moves of 2026-11-10 with the two checks a render makes of every move's take, and shows where each agrees with the labels. The cut check, `VT506`, is ffmpeg's scene score on every frame, failing over 0.3. The dissolve check, `VT507`, is the model's judge shown eight frames spread evenly across the take in one request, saying of each whether it shows two pictures overlaid, failing when any does. The project names the model whose judge judges, which `--model` overrides, and holds the ledger and the cache; Claude judges every model's moves but the stand-in's, whose own judge calls a frame overlaid when it is a blend of the clip's first and last frames, as a crossfade's middle frames are, and one picture when it shows a part of each, as a wipe's are.

The ten labelled moves live outside the repo, at `examples/surf_shop/` in the user's main checkout, which git ignores, or wherever `--clips` says; `LABELS` in the script names each by its path under that folder and the check a person says it fails. Wan 2.2 5B's five under `tape/moves/` all dissolve. Of Wan 3.0's under `tape-wan3/moves/`, `welcome--enter-shop` walks to the door and then dissolves, `entrance--i-have-an-account` pushes in and then cuts, `entrance--create-an-account` dissolves, and `entrance--browse-as-guest` and `signup--sign-up` whip past a surfboard, which counts as a cut. Their scene scores cost nothing: the seven dissolves score 0.086 to 0.187 and the three cuts 0.319 to 0.381, so 0.3 agrees with all ten. Their judgments are paid. `--fixture <file>` writes the judge's recorded answers to them, text only, and `tests/test_moves_test.py` replays them against frames sampled live; the answers hold only for the question they answered, so a changed prompt or rule needs a new paid calibration.

Before any call it prints the estimate: a judgment of each labelled clip by the judge, at the most each costs. It stops before any call when the most passes the cap, when no cap is set, or when the judge's API key is missing. The frames and the judge's replies go into `tapes/moves-test/calibration/` in the project, and every judgment into the project's `ledger.jsonl`, as a line of kind `judgment` naming the clip as its shot. Run again, it judges no clip whose answer is cached, so the report is rebuilt for free. It writes `tapes/moves-test/report.html`: each clip's eight frames with the judge's answer under each, its checks, where each check agrees with the labels, and the sources. `--model stand-in` runs the whole thing for free.

## The paid comparison of 2026-11-18 and 2026-11-19

Before the render drew keys this way, this script drew twelve moves of Contrail two ways in both stories and measured which way makes a move travel from one screen to the next as a camera would. `pinned` was how a render drew then: each base key on its own, text to video, each variant from its base's picture, and each move first-and-last-frame between its two keys. `rehearse` drew the entry's key as before and every other scene's base key as the move that first reaches it in the walk: image to video from the key of the state it is reached from, with a prompt composed of the move's clauses, `It ends held still on the next screen.`, and the state's own clauses without `Static shot.`, so the clip is the move and its last frame is the key. The estimate for gemini-omni-flash-1.2 was 108 to 216 calls, $47.47 to $94.93, with 68 to 136 reads at most $11.74 to $23.47 and 68 judgments at most $8.31, $67.51 to $126.72 in all. The paid run, under a cap the user raised to $70 and then $76, spent $74.66, with one fal HTTP 500 retaken. The calibration agreed 10 of 10 on both checks. Terminal: rehearse 7 of 12 moves travel, 6 of 12 keys pass, $16.99; pinned 4 of 12, with 3 cuts and whole-picture dissolves, 4 keys, $19.30. Posters: pinned 11 of 12, 4 keys, $19.51; rehearse 11 of 12, 3 keys, $18.38. One direction change drew 53 keys and 93 moves again under rehearse, 1 key and 12 moves under pinned. The user chose rehearse, which is now how `videotape render` draws every base but the entry's, and pinned is gone; only the calibration stayed in this script. The tapes of that run are under `examples/contrail/tapes/moves-test/pinned/` and `rehearse/` in the paid checkout, with their lines in its `ledger.jsonl`.

## Sub-features

- `moves-estimate` prints the model and its judge, the calibration line with the clips, their free scene scores and the judgments at the most they cost, and the most against the cap. `--estimate` stops there with exit 0 and needs no API key.
- `moves-over-cap` stops with `VT402` before the first call when the most passes `--cap-usd`, and names the cap the run needs.
- `moves-no-cap` stops with `VT402` before the first call when no `--cap-usd` is given.
- `moves-no-key` stops with `VT104` before any call when `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, which the judge needs, is missing.
- `moves-tests` is `uv run pytest tests/test_moves_test.py tests/test_checks.py tests/test_claude.py tests/test_standin.py tests/test_frames.py`, which judges the ten clips through a fake network that answers from the stand-in's judge: the estimate, the stops, the ledger, the rerun, the cap, the report, the fixture flag, and the two checks, the judge and the stand-in's judge on their own.
- `moves-calibration` is the two tests that hold the checks to the labels: `test_the_cut_check_agrees_with_the_labels_on_the_ten_clips` runs ffmpeg live on the clips, and `test_the_dissolve_check_agrees_with_the_labels_on_claudes_recorded_answers` replays `tests/fixtures/recorded/moves/calibration.json`. Both are skipped without the clips, and the second without the fixture.
- `moves-standin` is the free run with `--model stand-in` on a copy of examples/contrail, whose judge agrees with the labels on the three cuts and not on the dissolves, since a real dissolve's middle frames are no blend of the clip's two ends.
- `moves-rerun` runs the same command again after a run, and judges nothing.
- `moves-report` is what `report.html` must show.
- `moves-fixture` is `--fixture <file>` after the paid run, which writes Claude's recorded answers for the calibration test.
- `moves-paid` is the paid run on gemini-omni-flash-1.2's judge, Claude. Costs money, and waits for the user's cap.
- `moves-explain` is `videotape explain VT506` and `videotape explain VT507`.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- From the repo root, read the estimate first: `uv run scripts/moves-test examples/contrail --estimate`.
- Approve a cap at least the estimate's most, then run the same command with `--cap-usd <cap>` in place of `--estimate`. The cap stops a run before the judgment that would pass it. The run still writes the report from what was judged before it, shows a dissolve check the cap stopped as not judged, and exits 1 with `VT402`. A run again with a higher cap resumes.
- Open `examples/contrail/tapes/moves-test/report.html` from disk. It needs no server.
- After the paid run, run the same command again with `--fixture tests/fixtures/recorded/moves/calibration.json`, which judges nothing, writes the fixture, and lets `tests/test_moves_test.py` hold the dissolve check to the labels. Commit the fixture.
- Try it for free on a copy with `--model stand-in`, which needs no key and no cap, and `--clips <folder>` when the labelled clips are elsewhere.

## Gotchas

- The clips are not in the repo and must never be. A worktree needs the symlink above, which the main repo's `.git/info/exclude` hides from `git status`.
- The two labelled folders hold clips of one name, so a sampled frame is named by the clip's path under the clips folder, as in `tape--moves--entrance--browse-as-guest-0.png`.
- The judge's replies live under `tapes/moves-test/calibration/looks/` in the project and its judgments in the project's `ledger.jsonl`. After the paid run never delete them: a judgment deleted is a judgment paid for again.
- The fixture holds only the text of each reply, no frame, so `calibration.json` is small and carries no picture. A changed `JUDGE_PROMPT`, schema, settings or `FRAMES` changes `asked`, and the replay test then fails with what to do: run the paid calibration again with `--fixture`.
- The estimate charges every output token a judgment may take, so a judgment costs a fraction of its share, as a read does.
